How to pick a mower for a lawn you actually have

4 min read November 12, 2026 Dana Whitfield

Deck width, terrain and run time decide more than horsepower does. Before you compare spec sheets side by side, it helps to know which three numbers actually change what the machine feels like on your lawn.

Start with the deck, not the engine

A wider deck cuts more grass per pass, but it also has to fit between the shed door and the flower bed. On a lawn with two mature trees and a narrow side gate, a 21-inch deck will finish faster than a 30-inch one that can’t get through.

  • Under 2,000 sq ft — 18 to 20 inches is plenty, and lighter to push.
  • 2,000 to 6,000 sq ft — 21 to 24 inches is the sweet spot.
  • Over an acre — consider a ride-on before you consider a bigger deck.

What terrain changes

Slope is the one spec that isn’t on the box. A walk-behind with rear-wheel drive holds a 15-degree incline comfortably; front-wheel drive starts to slip well before that, especially on damp morning grass.

If you have to lean into the handle to keep traction, the machine is telling you something the brochure won’t.

Run time and the second battery

A 4.0 Ah pack on a 21-inch deck realistically covers about a third of an acre before the last quarter of the charge starts to feel slow. Two packs, charged in rotation, are usually cheaper than one bigger machine.

The short version

Match the deck to your gates, match the drive to your slope, and buy a second battery before you buy a bigger motor. Everything else is preference.
